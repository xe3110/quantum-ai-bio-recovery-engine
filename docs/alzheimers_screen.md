# Alzheimer's disease combination screen — protocol and results

A deterministic, multi-parameter, discovery-stage screen over a curated panel of
**36 Alzheimer's candidates** scored at **three orders** — 36 monotherapies, 630
pairs, 6,360 triples — against a **97-gene directional disease signature** and a
**cached STRING v12 interactome**.

It is **not** a clinical combination recommendation. Adding a chronic medication
to a very elderly, cognitively impaired patient carries adherence, falls,
bradycardia, imaging-monitoring, and caregiver costs that no in-silico score can
quantify, and the population often cannot report adverse effects reliably. Every
output is a hypothesis for experimental validation.

The standing procedure this run instantiates — what a registry entry must carry,
how the k-ary scorer is defined, and the checklist for adding the next disease —
is in the [disease campaign protocol](disease_campaign_protocol.md). **This
document states only what is specific to Alzheimer's.** The companion screens
are [multiple sclerosis](ms_publication_protocol.md) and
[Parkinson's](parkinsons_screen.md); the campaign that designs a molecule rather
than ranking existing ones is [here](denovo_design_protocol.md).

```bash
python -m experiments.alzheimers.run_combination_screen --top 25 --seed 7
python -m experiments.alzheimers.run_combination_screen --quick     # smoke run
pytest -q tests/test_combination_scoring.py tests/test_multi_disease.py
```

## 1. What is different about this disease

Parkinson's and MS each had a disease-defining protein or cell type that some
agent could reach. Alzheimer's is the hardest case so far, and it changes three
things in how the inputs have to be read.

| | Parkinson's | Alzheimer's |
|---|---|---|
| Defining pathology | α-synuclein, one disordered protein | amyloid-β **and** tau |
| Is it a transcript? | yes, `SNCA` | **amyloid-β is not** — it is a cleavage product |
| Disease-modifying agents that exist | none approved | anti-amyloid **antibodies**, approved, with imaging-detected toxicity |
| Dose-limiting toxicity | dyskinesia, impulse control | **ARIA** (brain oedema and microhaemorrhage), APOE4-dependent |
| Modalities in the panel | almost all small molecules | small molecules, five antibodies, one antisense oligonucleotide, peptides |

**Amyloid-β has no gene to score.** The signature is a transcript signature, and
amyloid burden is a protein-level quantity. The amyloid axis is therefore scored
through `APP`, the secretases (`BACE1`, `PSEN1`, `ADAM10`), and downstream
injury and glial markers (`NEFL`, `GFAP`, `C1QA`). For an antibody that acts on
the protein directly, the `APP` row is a **proxy for amyloid burden**, not a
measurement of anything the antibody does to the transcript. This is the largest
single reason the amyloid stratum should be read cautiously (§4).

## 2. Inputs specific to Alzheimer's

- **`data/ad_expression.csv`** — 97 genes across 13 pathways with `logFC`,
  curation `confidence`, and an explicit `desired_direction`.

  > **This signature is weaker evidence than the MS entry's.** It is curated
  > from literature knowledge rather than derived from a specific cohort, and
  > the registry's own `provenance` block says so. Two curation choices deserve
  > particular challenge: `GRIN1`/`GRIN2B` carry `desired_direction = −1` on a
  > gain-of-toxic-function reading of extrasynaptic NMDA signalling, although
  > bulk expression of both is reported inconsistently; and `TREM2`/`TYROBP`
  > are annotated **compensatory** with `desired_direction = +1`, which encodes
  > the hypothesis that boosting the protective microglial response helps — the
  > hypothesis the TREM2 agonist antibody AL002 then failed to confirm.

- **`data/drugs/alzheimers_panel_v1.json`** — 36 records regenerated by
  `python -m tools.curate_ad_panel`, which fails if any target gene is absent
  from the signature. 8 approved, 17 phase 3, 11 phase 2; 34 mechanism classes;
  62 of 97 signature genes targeted.

  The panel is weighted deliberately toward agents that **failed**. Alzheimer's
  has the longest record of failed disease-modification trials of any
  neurodegenerative disease, which makes it the strongest source of negative
  controls declared so far — five, against four for MS and five for Parkinson's.

- **`data/networks/string_ad_network.tsv`** — STRING v12, *H. sapiens*,
  confidence ≥ 0.4, 247 nodes, 10,572 edges. All 97 signature genes are present
  in the network, so **no gene alias is declared**; Parkinson's needed one
  (`GBA` → `GBA1`).

- **Vocabulary** — six therapeutic axes (`cholinergic_symptomatic`,
  `amyloid_modification`, `tau_modification`,
  `synaptic_excitotoxicity_protection`, `microglial_immune_modulation`,
  `metabolic_vascular_rescue`) and eight risk domains (`aria`,
  `bradycardia_syncope`, `cognitive_worsening`, `falls_sedation`,
  `infusion_hypersensitivity`, `cardiac`, `hepatic`, `gastrointestinal`).

  ARIA carries the highest risk weight because it is the dose-limiting toxicity
  of the only agents shown to modify the disease and it requires serial MRI.
  `cognitive_worsening` is next, because a drug that lowers the function it is
  meant to protect — as the BACE1 and γ-secretase inhibitors did — has failed
  regardless of its mechanism. **This vocabulary shares no therapeutic axis with
  either earlier disease**, and shares only generic organ toxicity (`cardiac`,
  `hepatic`, `gastrointestinal`) among risk domains. Tests assert both.

- **Delivery** — `requires_cns_exposure: true`, as for Parkinson's. The one
  place this needs care is the antibodies. Approved anti-amyloid antibodies
  reach the brain at roughly one per cent or less of plasma concentration and
  work by engaging plaque at that exposure, so the panel models them at CNS
  penetration 0.10–0.15 and compartment `both`, not at zero. **The result is
  sensitive to this choice**, and a test pins that the four amyloid antibodies
  stay in that band rather than being quietly moved to make a ranking look
  right.

## 3. Results — monotherapy versus combination

Compared on the efficacy block only. `priority_score` is **not** in this table
and must not be: it includes complementarity and separation terms a single agent
cannot earn by construction (see the [rule](disease_campaign_protocol.md#comparing-across-orders--the-rule)).

| order | n | median reversal | max reversal | % beating best monotherapy | max gain | % sub-additive | *p* |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 36 | 0.0163 | 0.0468 | — | — | 0% | — |
| 2 | 606 | 0.0325 | 0.0748 | 12.7% | +0.0280 | 28.1% | ~0 |
| 3 | 6,360 | 0.0488 | 0.1017 | 55.8% | +0.0549 | 62.0% | ~0 |

*p* is Mann-Whitney against the 36 monotherapy reversals, one-sided; it
underflows to 0.0 at these sample sizes and is reported as such. With thousands
of combinations against 36 singles a significant difference is close to
guaranteed and says nothing about whether it matters.

Best monotherapy by signed reversal is the **NLRP3 inhibitor exemplar** (0.0468)
— an agent whose evidence tier is extrapolated from other indications — followed
by semaglutide (0.0338), **donanemab** (0.0294, the best *approved*
disease-modifying agent), masitinib (0.0287), and pioglitazone (0.0264). By
*composite* score the top monotherapies are the cholinesterase inhibitors
(tacrine, rivastigmine, galantamine, donepezil), which is the composite doing
what it does: they are approved, low-uncertainty, and move few signature genes.

### Efficacy as percentages

The screen's efficacy figure is `signed_reversal`: the fraction of the
signature's total weight (1.0) that a regimen moves in the therapeutic
direction. Read as a percentage it is **the share of the disease signature
reversed**. It is not a share of patients helped, a slowing of decline, or any
clinical effect, and the inputs behind it are unitless hypotheses.

| | Alzheimer's | Parkinson's |
|---|---|---|
| Best monotherapy | **4.68%** — NLRP3 inhibitor exemplar (extrapolated evidence) | 5.19% — NLRP3 inhibitor |
| Best pair | **7.48%** — NLRP3 inhibitor exemplar + semaglutide | 10.00% |
| Best triple | **10.17%** — donanemab + NLRP3 inhibitor exemplar + semaglutide | 14.09% |

Restricted to **approved** agents only:

| | Signature reversed | Combination |
|---|---|---|
| Best single | 2.94% | donanemab |
| Best pair | 5.31% | rivastigmine + donanemab |
| Best triple | 6.48% | rivastigmine + memantine + donanemab |
| Other approved singles | 2.45% lecanemab, 2.37% rivastigmine, 2.01% tacrine, 1.76% donepezil, 1.54% galantamine, 1.43% aducanumab, 1.17% memantine | |

**As a share of what the panel could reach.** The raw percentage has no natural
scale, so it is also expressed against two ceilings
([tools/reversal_ceiling.py](../tools/reversal_ceiling.py)). The
**full-reversal ceiling** is the weight fraction of every signature gene the
panel targets at all (62 of 97 genes: **63.0%**), as if each were reversed
completely. The **pooled-panel ceiling** gives every agent at once, keeps only
therapeutic-direction effects, and combines them gene-wise under Bliss
(**39.5%**), which respects the curated effect sizes and is the more realistic
bound.

| | best reversal | % of full ceiling | % of pooled-panel ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alzheimer's k = 1 | 4.68% | 7.4% | 11.8% |
| Alzheimer's k = 2 | 7.48% | 11.9% | 18.9% |
| Alzheimer's k = 3 | 10.17% | 16.1% | 25.7% |
| Parkinson's k = 3 | 14.09% | 19.6% | 33.2% |
| MS k = 2 | 10.54% | 12.3% | 15.1% |
| MS k = 3 | 14.95% | 17.5% | 21.4% |
| Epilepsy k = 3 | 7.41% | 16.9% | 24.1% |

Read this way, the best Alzheimer's triple captures **about a quarter of what
its panel could reach** rather than "10%", and Alzheimer's sits below Parkinson's
at every order (25.7% against 33.2% at k = 3) on a lower ceiling (39.5% against
42.4%). Three cautions. Both ceilings are properties of the panel, and the panel
was curated from the signature's own gene set, so they are generous; a panel
built independently would reach less. This is a normalisation that makes the
diseases comparable with one another, not a calibration to clinical benefit.
And the ceilings are not targets: no regimen could combine every agent.

`reversal_efficiency` (therapeutic movement ÷ the signal an agent actually
engages) is 0.44 for the best single agent and 0.40 for the best triple: roughly
40–44% of the signature weight these agents engage is moved in the
therapeutic direction. All three headline combinations have
*q* < 0.05 against the permutation null. Absolute values are small because any
one regimen engages a handful of genes in a 97-gene signature.

**Recovery and P(Success) from the original screening CLI are not usable here.**
`python -m experiments.ms.screen_drugs --disease alzheimers` returns Recovery
0.955 and P(Success) 0.955 (95% CI 0.805–1.0) for *every* drug in its top ten,
including choline iodide, chlormequat chloride and 4-hydroxybenzaldehyde, next to
donepezil and memantine. The Parkinson's run does the same (0.961 for levodopa,
pramipexole, and PARAROSANILINE alike). The score has saturated and does not
discriminate, so neither figure is an efficacy estimate and neither should be
quoted. The likely cause is that this CLI builds a 12-node network from the
target list with no expression signature, but I did not investigate further. The
early MS numbers (Fingolimod 62.9% recovery, 66.7% P(Success)) came from a
version of the pipeline that did use expression data. The combination screen
above superseded this path for exactly that reason.

### Three readings that matter more than the leaderboard

**1. Combination gain is real but small in absolute terms, and smaller than in
Parkinson's at order 2.** The best pair recovers 0.0748 of a signature whose
total weight is 1.0, and only 12.7% of pairs beat their own best member
(Parkinson's: 26.4%). Read the ordering, not the magnitude: `target_effects` are
unitless directional hypotheses.

**2. Redundancy is much higher than in Parkinson's.** 28.1% of pairs and
**62.0%** of triples are sub-additive, against 12.8% and 36.0%. The likely cause
is that many agents in this panel converge on the same downstream inflammatory
transcripts (`TNF`, `IL1B`, `NFKB1`, `AIF1`), so agents with different
mechanisms still move the same genes. I have not tested that explanation.

**3. Third agents earn their place more often than in Parkinson's.** Of 6,360
triples, **2,097 (33.0%)** have a *negative* `score_gain_over_best_subset`, and
the median gain is **+0.0238**. Parkinson's was 60.3% negative with a median of
−0.0192. Readings 2 and 3 are not contradictory — triples here overlap more in the
transcripts they move, yet clear their best pair more often — but I have not
isolated why the regimen-cost and safety terms that sink Parkinson's triples
bite less here. One candidate is that the approved agents carry low burden in
the domains this disease weights; it is a hypothesis, not a finding.

The one approved combination in the panel, **donepezil + memantine** (marketed as
a fixed-dose product), ranks **70 of 606** pairs (top 12%, *q* = 0.10). It is a
sanity check, not a control: it sits in the upper decile without reaching the
leaderboard or *q* < 0.05, which is roughly what a screen that scores mechanism
rather than trial outcome should say about a combination whose benefit is modest.

## 4. Mechanism strata — the defensible result

**The unit of inference is the mechanism stratum, not the named combination.**

Bootstrap resampling under the curated target-effect uncertainty gives a top-25
Jaccard of **0.187 at order 2** and **0.085 at order 3**, with rank 95% CIs
spanning tens to hundreds of positions (Parkinson's: 0.238 / 0.224). The
instability is worse here, and the runner prints its explicit warning at both
orders. Weight sensitivity is again excellent (Spearman 0.969 / 0.966 mean under
±50% weight jitter), so the instability is in the *inputs*, not the weighting.

Stratum medians at order 2:

| axis stratum | n | median | 95% CI | q |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| cholinergic_symptomatic + microglial_immune_modulation | 61 | 1.139 | [1.055, 1.225] | ~0 |
| microglial_immune_modulation + synaptic_excitotoxicity_protection | 25 | 1.112 | [1.021, 1.202] | 0.0012 |
| cholinergic_symptomatic + metabolic_vascular_rescue | 70 | 1.110 | [1.031, 1.189] | ~0 |
| cholinergic_symptomatic + synaptic_excitotoxicity_protection | 19 | 1.103 | [0.973, 1.203] | 0.0016 |
| cholinergic_symptomatic + tau_modification | 54 | 1.096 | [1.016, 1.181] | ~0 |
| … | | | | |
| tau_modification + tau_modification | 26 | 0.951 | [0.774, 1.109] | 1 |
| metabolic_vascular_rescue + metabolic_vascular_rescue | 45 | 0.919 | [0.809, 1.043] | 1 |
| **amyloid_modification + amyloid_modification** | 37 | **0.894** | [0.785, 1.027] | 1 |

**Six of the top seven strata contain `cholinergic_symptomatic`**, and the
bottom is occupied by same-axis pairings with amyloid-only last.

**This is the opposite of what Parkinson's and MS showed, and it should not be
smoothed over.** In both earlier screens the strongest strata paired something
with the axis that had the *least* approved chemical matter (trophic support in
Parkinson's, remyelination in MS), and the design campaign's independent
axis-gap analysis pointed at the same axis. Here the strongest strata pair
something with the axis that is *already served* — the cholinesterase
inhibitors — and the design campaign's gap analysis points elsewhere (§7). The
two analyses answer different questions and, for the first time, disagree.

Why the composite favours the cholinergic axis is visible in the inputs, and is
a property of the scorer as much as of the biology:

- the cholinergic agents are approved (full evidence credit) with the lowest
  `target_uncertainty` in the panel (0.10–0.20);
- the anti-amyloid antibodies carry the highest-weighted risk domain (ARIA,
  0.8–0.95 burden), model at CNS penetration ≤ 0.15, and move the signature
  only through the `APP` proxy (§1).

Whether that is a correct reading of a risky, poorly-exposed class or an
artefact of scoring a protein-level mechanism with a transcript-level
signature, **this screen cannot tell.** That is the honest limit on the
amyloid-modification result.

## 5. Controls, declared before ranking

| Control | Members | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Positive redundancy | Donepezil + Rivastigmine | **Excluded as expected** (both cholinesterase inhibitors) |
| Safety penalty | Tacrine (62), Aducanumab (137), Semagacestat (295) | **None in the pooled top 25 — but see below** |
| Negative efficacy | Pioglitazone (**6**), Verubecestat (**23**), Atorvastatin (55), Solanezumab (131), Naproxen (215) | **Two of five reach the top 25** |

Ranks are the best position in the pooled primary ranking of orders 2 and 3, as
the runner defines them.

24 of 630 pairs were excluded as redundant. Twenty-one share a safety class
(6 among the four cholinesterase inhibitors, 6 among the four anti-amyloid
antibodies, 6 among the four kinase inhibitors, 3 among the infusion-reaction
antibodies), and 12 exceed the target-overlap limit, 9 of which also share a
safety class — for example the anti-tau antibody and the *MAPT* antisense
oligonucleotide, both aimed at `MAPT`. No triple was excluded, for the reason
given in the Parkinson's protocol: the order ladder catches redundancy one order
down.

**The safety-penalty control did not behave the way its result line suggests.**
By the runner's definition — best position in the pooled primary ranking —
Tacrine is at rank 62 and the control passes. Read per order it does not:

- Tacrine is **#1 of 36 monotherapies by composite score**, ahead of
  rivastigmine, although it has the **highest safety union of any single agent**
  (0.203; hepatic burden 0.95);
- it is #17 of 606 primary pairs, and appears **three times** in the
  diversity-capped top-25 pairs and **three times** in the top-25 triples.

Tacrine was withdrawn for hepatotoxicity. The screen has no idea that it was
withdrawn: `evidence_tier` is `approved`, so it earns full evidence credit, its
cholinesterase effects are strong, and the hepatic burden is diluted by
averaging across eight risk domains. A single catastrophic domain is a weak
signal in a weighted mean. I have **not** tuned the scorer to make this control
pass, because that would be fitting the method to its own check; it is reported
as a failure of the method to represent withdrawal. A follow-up worth making is a
max-domain term in `safety_union`, evaluated on all three diseases at once.

**Pioglitazone and verubecestat reaching the top 25 are reported failures, not
filtered ones.** Both failed phase 3. They appear because the screen scores
mechanism and transcriptional direction, not trial outcome, and both have a
coherent mechanism (PPAR-γ agonism moves insulin-signalling and inflammatory
transcripts; BACE1 inhibition removes the amyloidogenic cleavage). Verubecestat
is the sharper case: it *worsened* cognition in phase 3, a harm the
`cognitive_worsening` risk domain is meant to capture but which is priced at 0.6
of a weight-0.9 domain, averaged. **Mechanistic coherence is not evidence of
clinical benefit**, and two of five negative controls surviving is a worse
record than Parkinson's one of five.

## 6. Known limitations specific to Alzheimer's

The [standing limitations](disease_campaign_protocol.md#7-standing-limitations-of-every-campaign)
of every campaign apply. What is different here:

- **The signature cannot see the defining protein** (§1). The amyloid result is
  the least reliable in the screen, and it is the result most people will look
  for first.
- **Withdrawal is invisible to the score.** Tacrine (hepatotoxicity) and
  aducanumab (withdrawn by its sponsor in 2024) score as ordinary approved
  agents. See §5.
- **ARIA risk is genotype-dependent and the model is not.** The imaging
  toxicity that defines the disease-modifying class is concentrated in *APOE4*
  carriers, and `safety_burden` is a single population-level number per agent.
  A genotype-stratified analysis is required before any antibody combination is
  read as a candidate.
- **No pharmacokinetic or drug–drug-interaction model.** This gap binds hard
  here. Cholinesterase inhibitors are opposed pharmacologically by anything
  anticholinergic, add to bradycardia and syncope with rate-lowering drugs, and
  the population is frail and polypharmacic. `regimen_burden` captures route and
  half-life spread only. It remains the top-priority next model.
- **Symptomatic and disease-modifying agents are ranked on one scale**, as in
  Parkinson's, and more consequentially: the axis that leads the strata is the
  symptomatic one.
- **The order ladder is greedy.** A triple whose members are individually weak
  but jointly excellent is unreachable if one of its pairs was excluded as
  redundant. This bites particularly here: the redundancy rule removes every
  antibody–antibody pair among the amyloid agents, and every kinase-inhibitor
  pair, including plausible dual-hit combinations such as tideglusib with
  saracatinib on the tau axis.
- **The `NLRP3 inhibitor (class exemplar)` is the best monotherapy by reversal
  and rests on extrapolated evidence.** It names a chemotype, not a drug, and
  its tier was assigned as it was for Parkinson's. Do not read it as a
  finding.
- **Two negative-trial records are recent and should be confirmed against the
  primary publications before they are cited**: semaglutide not slowing
  progression in phase 3 (late 2025) and AL002 missing its phase 2 endpoint.
  Both are recorded in the panel from memory of the announcements, not from a
  source file.

## 7. Where this screen ends and design begins

The design campaign runs over the same Alzheimer's disease model. Its axis-gap
analysis, derived from the panel's *approved* agents rather than from any
combination score, reports:

| therapeutic axis | unmet fraction |
|---|---|
| cholinergic_symptomatic | 0.00 |
| amyloid_modification | 0.25 |
| microglial_immune_modulation | 0.50 |
| synaptic_excitotoxicity_protection | 0.75 |
| **tau_modification** | **1.00** |
| **metabolic_vascular_rescue** | **1.00** |

Two axes are entirely unserved by approved agents: tau and metabolic/vascular
rescue. Amyloid is the one disease-modifying axis with approved cover (0.25).

The screen and the gap analysis **do not converge** in this disease (§4): the
screen's strongest strata involve the served cholinergic axis, and neither of
the unserved axes appears at the top. That is a result about this disease and
this panel, and I would rather report a disagreement than pick the reading that
resembles the earlier two.

python -m experiments.design.run_denovo_design \

  --outdir experiments/design/results/alzheimers

The design results are in [§8 of the design protocol](denovo_design_protocol.md#alzheimers-disease).
The headline is a caution, not a candidate: the profile's highest-priority

reaches it is too polar for the CNS envelope.

## 8. Reproduce

```bash
python -m tools.curate_ad_panel                                    # regenerate + validate the panel
python -m tools.fetch_string_network --signature data/ad_expression.csv \
    --out data/networks/string_ad_network.tsv                      # optional; cached copy is committed
python -m experiments.alzheimers.run_combination_screen --top 25 --seed 7
pytest -q tests/
```

Outputs land in `experiments/alzheimers/results/`:
`ad_combination_screen.json` (full report with provenance) and
`ad_combinations_full.csv` (7,026 rows — every combination at every order,
including the ones excluded from the primary ranking).

Every run is deterministic under a stated seed. The provenance block records a
**content digest of each input file**, the git revision **and whether the tree
was dirty**, Python and package versions, the chemistry backend, the seed, and
the exact command. `--quick` runs a reduced-draw smoke test in about a minute;
the full run takes roughly 6–7 minutes, most of it the order-3 permutation null
and bootstrap.

## 9. Required validation before publication

1. Pre-register stage (MCI, mild, moderate), outcome (CDR-SB, ADAS-Cog,
   amyloid PET), and weights before looking at rankings.
2. Replace the signature with a discovery and an independent validation cohort;
   analyse by *APOE* genotype and by amyloid/tau biomarker status separately,
   since the panel contains agents that act on each.
3. Re-derive `target_effects` from measured pharmacology, and give the
   protein-level agents a scoring path that does not run through a transcript
   proxy. The rank instability in §4 is driven by `target_uncertainty`, and
   reducing it is the highest-value fix available.
4. **Build the PK/DDI model** (§6) before any combination here is proposed for
   in-vivo work, and add a max-domain safety term so withdrawal-grade toxicity
   cannot be averaged away (§5).
5. Test dose matrices in disease-relevant human systems — iPSC-derived neurons
   and microglia co-culture, with amyloid and tau challenge. Report raw
   response, dose-response fits, and Bliss, HSA, Loewe, and ZIP, not a model
   score alone.
6. Validate top strata in an appropriate model (APP/PS1 or 5xFAD for amyloid,
   PS19 for tau), assessing exposure, PK, and PD biomarkers.
7. Release code, frozen inputs, seed, full rankings including excluded
   combinations, and the control analysis.

## Where quantum computing is and is not used

**Not used: the combination screen.** The k = 1, 2, 3 screen (36 monotherapies, 630 pairs, 6,360 triples) is classical.

*Why.* (1) The screen enumerates every combination exhaustively, so the answer is exact
and there is no search problem for a quantum heuristic to help with; the full run takes about six minutes.
(2) The expensive part is statistical scoring (the permutation null, bootstrap stability,
weight sensitivity), which is classical and has no quantum counterpart here. (3) The
drug-selection problem can be written as a QUBO (the project does this, see below), but at
this size exact enumeration is faster and gives the true optimum, so there is nothing for
a quantum solver to improve on. Quantum optimisation could matter for much larger
selections (many agents, high order); none of the panels here is in that regime.

**Used, as a benchmark only: the design campaign's fragment selection.** The 10-variable arm-selection QUBO gives +0.0724 on all three solvers; the depth sweep is d1 +0.0404, d2 to d4 +0.0724, **d5 +0.0404**, so deeper was worse at depth 5.
Enumeration finishes in about 0.07 s; the QAOA depth sweep takes 5 to 6 s. The arm sets
carried into molecule assembly come from **enumeration**, not from QAOA, and QAOA's output
is not used downstream.

*Why only a benchmark.* At 10 binary variables there are 1,024 states, which exhaustive
search covers instantly, so no quantum advantage can be demonstrated or is expected. The
benchmark checks that the Hamiltonian formulation is faithful and transfers to a quantum
algorithm, and that is all it shows.

**Never used: real quantum hardware, and molecule assembly.** QAOA ran on a classical
simulator (Qiskit Aer), and the assembly step that builds the new structures is a classical
stochastic search. None of the findings in this document depends on quantum computing.
